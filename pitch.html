Strategy · M1

The growth bet

Hypothesis

The core belief

FinWise's biggest growth problem is that growth depends entirely on paid acquisition, while only about 2% of trials convert to paid regardless of spend or engagement, because trial users come in alone through paid channels and use FinWise as a single-player tool, even though small-business finance involves accountants, bookkeepers and partners.
The bet

Where I focus

The highest-leverage experiment I'd run first is a collaboration loop that prompts trial users to invite their accountant or bookkeeper after their first data import, A/B tested over 6 weeks with trial-to-paid conversion as the primary metric. [Note - the rest of the modules didn't really seem to focus on this type of collaboration loop, so the presentation seems a bit disjointed - I am going to give Product School some feedback on this]
Activation · M2

The Aha moment

Aha moment

When value lands

“I can see my real cash position, and forecast where it’s heading, without having built a spreadsheet.” In practice, that’s the first time a user sees their own bank transactions auto-categorized into a cash-in/cash-out view, with a short forward forecast, such as “projected balance in 30 days.”
Retention · M3

The habit mechanic

Mechanic

The habit loop

FinWise’s churn appears to come from two sources: customers try financial modeling but don’t get forecasts they trust, and the product has turned into a quick check-in tool rather than somewhere real work gets done. The Forecast Confidence meter turns those short, low-value check-ins into a two-minute weekly habit that keeps the user’s forecast accurate. That speaks to the data showing modeling usage nearly doubling (25% to 47%) while sessions got shorter and churn rose by about half. Because users can see that a reliable forecast depends on their reviewed transactions, the feature makes modeling trustworthy and gives them a concrete reason to keep coming back, and to renew.
Metrics · M4

What I measure

Leading

Predicts outcomes

- Get Started Import Feature Usage %: This provides evidence that users are successfully connecting their bank info
- Financial Modeling Feature Usage %: This provides evidence that users are exploring the modeling, which is part of our North Star
Lagging

The outcome

- Trial-to-Paid Conversion Rate: Users see enough value to continue paying after the trial
Data pattern

What it revealed

There's no correlation between trial-to-paid conversion and the two leading indicators I chose, Import feature usage % and Financial modeling feature usage %. If activation through these features were the main thing holding conversion back, all that variation should have shown up somewhere in conversion. Revised hypothesis: FinWise’s conversion problem isn’t mainly about reaching features. Many trial users import data and use modeling, but that use doesn’t turn into a reason to pay. Either these features aren’t the moment users see real value, or something after that moment blocks conversion: price versus perceived value, trial length, or the paywall itself.
Experiment · M5

How I test it

Method

Test design

Method: Multi-Armed Bandit. Why: FinWise can't afford to split equal traffic for a certain duration and wait to see the outcomes, because we are optimizing for trial to paid conversion while improving retention. We therefore want to minimize dropout during the test.
Hypothesis

Metric + success

If we improve the quality of the financial forecasts (and therefore value) for FinWise users, then we expect to see improved trial-to-paid conversion and improved retention.

Primary metric: Weekly active sessions per user, target: A 15% increase in weekly active sessions within two weeks
Guardrails

Limits + read date

Guardrail: Financial data import completion rate should not change, since the change targets what happens after activation. Read date: 14 days after launch, on the 15th, no earlier.
Monetization · M6

The model

Stage 1 · Value Creation, Trial-to-paid conversion is only 2% and 60% of paying customers churn within a year - so we haven't demonstrated that users are getting enough value yet. Model Recommendation: Freemium.
Recommendation

The move

Move from a reverse trial to a freemium model: every new user gets a basic free version that allows them to organize their finances. The advanced features like financial modeling and scenario planning are gated and require a paid subscription.

The pricing bet: Packaging matched to value, validating that the free/paid line sits where users feel they need more.
Model signal

What success looks like

This targets the Financial Modeling Feature Usage % activation indicator - going beyond a certain amount of usage requires a paid subscription.
Reflection · Capstone

What I learned

Friction

The hard part

The data fought my starting hypothesis. I expected conversion to rise with activation, but it held at about 2% for 13 months while Import and Modeling usage swung by 15–47 points. Import showed no correlation with conversion (r = −0.05), and Modeling only a weak, non-significant one (r = 0.24). The hard part was letting go of the story I’d brought in, and accepting that monthly totals could weaken a user-level claim but not settle it.
Aha

The moment it clicked

When I realized that 40–58% of users were already reaching the “activation” features while conversion stayed flat, the problem moved. It wasn’t getting users in the door; it was what happened after. That changed the question from “how do we activate more users?” to “where is the moment worth paying for?” The answer was to put the paywall at Modeling instead of at a trial deadline.
Takeaways

What stuck

Experimentation works as a loop: bet, indicator, evidence, revised bet, test design, model. The pricing model comes out of what the tests show.
